# Choice previews use shared rule plans and authoritative spatial targets

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-10-09

Choice Preview derives from the executable semantics of shared rule primitives. Cards can migrate opaque purchase-path or scheduled-offer rules into those primitives, but must not maintain a separate presentation implementation of the same effects. Original Payment Path identity binds a purchase outcome even when discounts change the resources actually paid. Shared plan composition preserves effect order and optionality rather than guessing consequences from text, card IDs, or opaque choice values.

The generic preview layer may project declared deterministic storage operations onto a detached copy and read existing source-card scoring queries. It reports the current source-card scoring contribution change, not a prediction of the entire branch's final score. It does not run arbitrary card callbacks, advance a cloned Engine through later choices, or change admission decisions. Future schedules use the same target filtering as execution and distinguish received resources, optional actions, conditional rewards, and purchase offers. Shared scheduling primitives own preview attribution while existing queue execution semantics remain intact.

Farm target choices carry backend-declared legal targets and opaque selection bindings. Players select ordinary targets directly on their farm, and Card Field targets on their displayed crop slots. Multiple slots belonging to one Logical Field retain one field identity. A target with more than one legal alternative requires an explicit alternative selection; the client never picks the first binding or derives legality from card-specific strings.

The rejected alternatives were card-owned preview callbacks, a second card-specific presentation registry, and general speculative execution of existing callbacks. The first two duplicate rule knowledge; the third adds isolation, hidden-information, and execution-budget concerns without providing an inspectable shared rule contract. See ADR-0015 for current-step admission, ADR-0018 for Future Schedule target identity, ADR-0020 for hidden round information, and ADR-0023 for explicit state visibility.

## Action and future-effect presentation

The 2026-10-13 implementation extends this decision to the native anytime census, executable option annotations, seasonal actions, card-created action spaces, current payment plans, and all explicitly scheduled or activated timed effects. The source audit records 48 native anytime entries, 205 annotation occurrences, 85 schedule-primitive sources and 219 phase-hook candidates. These are audit rows, not independently validated effects: standing abilities, immediate flows and lifecycle cleanup remain outside future-board scheduling. Closed source-local plans replace opaque native consequences, and one generic renderer consumes their recursive public descriptions. Pure factories do not receive event or protected-observation emitters. Private target previews follow the interaction audience in both idle and waiting states.

The same rule-plan ownership applies to all executable action and option descriptions, including anytime entries and payment or exchange choices. Static card names, target names and semantic branch names remain presentation metadata. Costs, quantities, timing and declared conditions come from executable rule semantics rather than a separately maintained card-specific explanation. Printed card rules remain authored rule text.

Action descriptions preserve the full declared flow recursively, including sequence, exclusive alternatives, inclusive choices, parallel effects and optional steps. They describe unresolved quantities or targets as choices instead of inventing a selected value. Internal bookkeeping remains absent from player-facing descriptions. Complete nested effects are displayed rather than replaced by a short independent summary.

Round-space presentation separates future effects by receiving player and identifies each player's area with that player's color. Within a player's area, effects with different sources or different complete contents remain distinct. Only effects with the same source, target round, reward, costs and conditions may share one displayed group, which preserves their multiplicity. Display grouping never deduplicates execution. Scheduled optional actions and purchase offers are included alongside future receives and explicitly retain their optionality, payment and conditions.

The board shows already scheduled or activated timed effects, including effects due later in the current round, at the next harvest or in a particular future phase. Standing every-round and every-receive abilities remain on their cards rather than being copied onto every round. Each round retains separate colored player areas with distinct effect markers and exact quantities; selecting that round opens the complete source-attributed, recursive description with multiplicity, payment and conditions. Timing is part of effect identity, so round-start and return-home effects cannot collapse into one group.

Action-space icon descriptions and tooltips describe base rules from shared rule data. Executable options describe the current Interaction Player's actual costs and effects, including modifiers and their unresolved choices. Switching the Farm View Player does not change that rule actor. Baking recipes are supplied by the same authoritative trade rules used by execution rather than copied into a frontend rate table.

Opaque timed callbacks must migrate to declared executable plans before their effects can be completely described. A presentation-only callback that re-creates their reward or trigger is not an acceptable bridge. Shared plans preserve timing, actor, optionality and conditions, and execution and description consume the same plan. Public schedule projection includes only explicit player-facing facts and never serializes raw card state, callbacks or action contexts. Hidden hands and unrevealed action-space identity remain protected.

## Action and schedule regression scenarios

These repairs use Session tests because they involve payment, choices, deferred rewards, multiple players and nested flows. Algorithm tests supplement the Session boundary, while browser tests verify visible descriptions and board interaction. Every ordinary scenario begins with a fresh seeded two-player work-phase Session using `createWorkSession`; both players have inert hands `['__test_placeholder__']`, two workers at home, empty played zones and card states, wooden two-room houses, no fields or future entries, and unoccupied action spaces. Baseline inventory is 20 each of food, wood, clay, reed and stone, with zero crops and animals. Only the explicit overrides below are applied before loading the fixture. The fixed M131 fixture has 24 choices with four offers each. On the local ARM64 host, 30 seeded purchase commands (including preview generation, scoring and response assembly) measured a median of 4.46 ms and p95 of 13.75 ms. This is a local command measurement, not a production transport or browser latency claim. Browser coverage uses a one-player Moor fixture only for the multiple-physical-card binding case; ordinary fixtures remain two-player.
