# Chinese Twenty-Four Solar Terms Illustration Art Direction

> **Project Positioning**
>
> This is a series of 24 horizontal illustrations themed around the Chinese Twenty-Four Solar Terms, intended for website backgrounds, banners, or feature-page headers.
>
> The overall aesthetic references the warm farming narrative associated with the board game **Agricola**. **Klemens Franz**, the original illustrator of Agricola, is explicitly acknowledged as a visual reference for this project.
>
> The project must be an **original reinterpretation of Chinese farming culture**. It must not copy any existing Agricola card, character, building, scene, or specific composition.

## 1. Overall Project Goals

These are not 24 unrelated “Chinese-style rural illustrations.” Together they form:

> **A continuous story of rural life and farming across the Twenty-Four Solar Terms, set in the same Chinese village, centered on the same family of five, and grounded in the same fields.**

### 1.1 Intended Result

- Establish clear **series-wide consistency**;
- Create a strong **sense of time passing**;
- Present specific and credible **farming narratives**;
- Keep each banner **balanced and readable**;
- Maintain unmistakable **Chinese rural cultural identity**;
- Preserve the core qualities associated with Agricola and Klemens Franz:
  - Warmth and simplicity;
  - A hand-drawn quality;
  - Farming-centered storytelling;
  - Small human figures;
  - Pen linework combined with transparent watercolor.

### 1.2 Core Narrative

Across the 24 images, the series shows:

1. How a family of five adjusts its work with each solar term;
2. How the same fields move from thawing and sowing through growth, harvest, and fallow;
3. How the same village experiences rain, heat, dew, frost, and snow;
4. How family members cooperate, learn, harvest, and gather throughout the year;
5. How Major Cold leads back into the next Start of Spring, completing the farming cycle.

## 2. Core Visual Principles

### 2.1 Style Direction

The series should combine:

- The pastoral board-game illustration character associated with Agricola;
- A traditional Chinese rural worldview;
- Fine, natural pen lines with slight hand-drawn irregularity;
- Transparent watercolor washes with restrained layered color;
- Ivory cold-press watercolor paper texture;
- Soft, even, natural lighting;
- Small but clearly readable human figures;
- A warm, rustic, calm atmosphere rich in everyday narrative;
- Horizontal middle- and long-distance compositions suitable for website backgrounds.

### 2.2 Fixed Style Keywords

The following terms may remain in every prompt:

```text
hand-drawn pen linework
transparent watercolor washes
ivory watercolor paper texture
pastoral farming board-game illustration
traditional Chinese countryside
warm and rustic
small human figures
middle-distance narrative scene
website banner background
balanced distribution
consistent series
natural-pigment colors
slightly uneven handmade coloring
```

### 2.3 Originality Boundary

The visual reference establishes tone, not content to copy. The series must:

- Not copy any existing Agricola card;
- Not reproduce existing character designs;
- Not reproduce a specific combination of buildings;
- Not reproduce an existing scene layout;
- Not imitate a specific published composition by Klemens Franz;
- Build an independent world from the Twenty-Four Solar Terms, Chinese farming, and Chinese rural life.

## 3. Recurring Family of Five

This is the most important continuity requirement in the project.

### 3.1 Family Structure

The entire series centers on **the same Chinese farming family**, with exactly five recurring protagonists:

1. **Father**;
2. **Mother**;
3. **Elder daughter**;
4. **Son**;
5. **Younger daughter**.

> The fixed age order of the children is: **elder daughter oldest, son in the middle, younger daughter youngest.**

### 3.2 Non-Negotiable Requirements

They are the **recurring protagonists** of the series, not random villagers regenerated for each image.

Keep the following stable:

- Family structure;
- Gender;
- Age and height hierarchy;
- Hairstyles, clothing style, and primary clothing colors;
- Clear family relationships;
- Immediate recognition of the same family across all 24 images.

### 3.3 Family Roles

#### Father

- Adult man;
- One of the household’s main workers;
- Primarily responsible for:
  - Plowing;
  - Carrying shoulder-pole loads;
  - Harvesting;
  - Repairing farm tools;
  - Moving grain;
  - Caring for the water buffalo or ox;
  - Heavier tasks such as repairing fences, stacking firewood, and reinforcing the roof.

#### Mother

- Adult woman;
- One of the household’s main workers;
- Primarily responsible for:
  - Sowing;
  - Transplanting rice seedlings;
  - Picking tea;
  - Tending the vegetable garden;
  - Gathering and drying crops;
  - Preparing winter stores;
  - Caring for chickens, ducks, and geese;
  - Organizing courtyard work and household routines.

#### Elder Daughter

- The oldest child;
- Skilled enough to take part in regular farm work;
- Primarily responsible for:
  - Helping her mother harvest;
  - Carrying light produce;
  - Organizing the drying yard;
  - Carrying baskets and passing tools;
  - Watching over her younger siblings;
  - Bundling, drying, and packing crops during the autumn harvest.

#### Son

- The middle child;
- Often follows his father to learn farm work;
- Primarily responsible for:
  - Passing tools;
  - Carrying small bundles of wood or straw;
  - Helping drive chickens and ducks;
  - Inspecting ridges, ditches, and paddies;
  - Learning plowing, irrigation, and harvesting from his father;
  - Performing light outdoor work.

#### Younger Daughter

- The youngest child;
- Performs only light supporting tasks;
- Primarily responsible for:
  - Feeding chickens and ducks;
  - Carrying a small bamboo basket;
  - Gathering vegetables or fallen fruit;
  - Passing small objects;
  - Following other family members;
  - Reinforcing the warmth and everyday life of the family.

### 3.4 Fixed Visual Anchors

Use stable visual anchors to reduce character drift between images:

| Family member | Fixed clothing colors | Build and behavior anchors |
|---|---|---|
| Father | Dark blue or indigo short jacket | Sturdy and calm; usually associated with tools, the draft animal, firewood, or heavy labor |
| Mother | Warm brown or bean-green top | Gentle and steady; often carries an apron, headscarf, bamboo basket, or garden tool |
| Elder daughter | Pale green or light blue | Tallest child; capable movements; actively assists her mother |
| Son | Pale earth yellow, light brown, or gray-blue | Middle height; often near his father; curious and learning |
| Younger daughter | Pale pink, beige, or soft apricot | Smallest figure; lively movements; often near poultry, a small basket, or fallen fruit |

### 3.5 Clothing Principles

Use generalized traditional Chinese rural workwear without tying it to a specific dynasty:

- Short front-opening jackets;
- Loose trousers;
- Cloth leg wraps;
- Bamboo hats;
- Straw rain capes;
- Cloth headscarves;
- Padded jackets;
- Cloth shoes or straw sandals.

Avoid:

- Court dress;
- Opera costumes;
- Studio-style historical costumes;
- Martial-arts outfits;
- Fantasy cultivation clothing;
- Modern clothing or footwear.

### 3.6 Appearance Rules

- Not every image must show all five people;
- Most images should show **three to five family members**;
- Major spring, summer, and autumn work periods should show all five whenever practical;
- Grain in Ear, Autumn Equinox, Winter Solstice, and Major Cold should be key family-group scenes;
- If a family member is absent, do not add a random figure who could be mistaken for a new protagonist;
- If background villagers are necessary, keep them few, low-detail, and clearly subordinate to the family.

### 3.7 Year-Round Family Participation

- **Spring:** the parents lead spring fieldwork while the children observe, learn, and assist;
- **Summer:** work is busiest; the elder daughter and son take on more responsibility while the younger daughter receives safe, light tasks;
- **Autumn:** the whole family harvests, carries, dries, sorts, and stores crops; group activity is richest;
- **Winter:** outdoor work decreases and shifts toward the home, animal shed, woodshed, granary, and kitchen;
- **Winter Solstice:** emphasize the warmth of the family gathering;
- **Major Cold:** the family prepares seeds and tools for the next Start of Spring.

## 4. Shared World Setting

### 4.1 Regional Character

Set the series in an imagined **Jiangnan–southern Anhui hill-country farming village**.

This setting naturally accommodates:

- Rice paddies;
- Wheat fields;
- Rapeseed fields;
- Tea slopes;
- Vegetable gardens;
- Low green hills;
- Irrigation streams;
- White walls and gray roof tiles;
- A rammed-earth granary;
- Bamboo fences;
- A stone arch bridge;
- A wooden waterwheel;
- A water buffalo or ox;
- Chickens, ducks, and geese;
- A lotus pond;
- Persimmon trees;
- Chrysanthemums;
- Reeds;
- Frost and snow;
- Wintersweet blossoms.

The village need not correspond precisely to a real province. It is an artistically distilled ideal farming village with an unmistakable Chinese identity.

### 4.2 Recurring Landmarks

Keep these landmarks recognizable across all 24 images while changing their seasonal state:

1. **Left middle ground:** a cluster of white-walled, gray-tiled farmhouses and a rammed-earth granary;
2. **Slightly left of center:** a small stone arch bridge crossing an irrigation stream;
3. **Beside the farmhouses:** a wooden waterwheel serving as a recurring visual anchor;
4. **Foreground:** field ridges, stone slabs, or a compacted-earth path;
5. **Distance:** tea slopes, fields, and layered low hills;
6. **Left foreground:** a fruit, plum, or persimmon tree changing with the seasons;
7. **Beside the stream:** a willow showing spring buds, summer shade, autumn yellow, and bare winter branches;
8. **Courtyard:** the family’s bamboo-fenced yard, woodpile, granary, and poultry area.

### 4.3 Recurring Animals

Repeat the following throughout the year:

- One water buffalo or ox;
- Several chickens;
- Two or three ducks or geese;
- A few seasonal birds, such as swallows or migrating geese;
- Insects only as small details, never exaggerated close-ups.

Animal appearances should remain stable, especially the draft animal, which should read as the same family asset throughout the series.

### 4.4 Reusable Chinese Rural Elements

- White-walled, gray-tiled farmhouses;
- Rammed-earth granary;
- Bamboo fences;
- Small stone bridge;
- Stone-slab or compacted-earth paths;
- Wooden waterwheel;
- Shoulder pole;
- Bamboo baskets;
- Winnowing basket;
- Bamboo sieves and drying trays;
- Grain baskets;
- Straw rain cape;
- Bamboo hat;
- Ceramic storage jars;
- Woodpile;
- Tea-picking baskets;
- Bamboo broom;
- Reeds;
- Lotus pond;

## 5. Shared Composition System

### 5.1 Aspect Ratio

Use consistently:

- Horizontal orientation;
- **16:9**;
- Suitable for website banners, home-page backgrounds, and feature-page headers.

### 5.2 Camera Language

Use consistently:

- A slightly elevated view;
- Middle- to long-distance framing;
- Clear foreground, middle-ground, and distant layers;
- No close-up portraits;
- No cinematic shallow depth of field;
- No exaggerated wide-angle perspective;
- No extreme high-angle or drone view.

### 5.3 Layout

Use a horizontally balanced structure with controlled density:

- **Left, center, and right:** each contains recognizable village, field, human, or natural elements, preventing visual weight from collecting on one side;
- **Lower half:** fields, ridges, tools, and farming activities unfold in separate groups instead of one crowded cluster;
- **Middle ground:** houses, trees, the waterwheel, bridge, and stream connect the narrative groups;
- **Upper half:** sky, distant hills, and tree canopies balance the whole image without creating artificial empty space;
- **Distance:** low-contrast hills, tea slopes, or terraced fields continue the horizontal layers.

### 5.4 Suggested Spatial Distribution

```text
left 0%–33%: village structures, trees, or one family activity group
center 33%–67%: main farm work, stream, stone bridge, or waterwheel
right 67%–100%: fields, animals, supporting activity, or seasonal nature
lower 35%–100%: grouped fields, people, animals, and tools
upper 0%–35%: naturally connected sky, distant hills, and tree canopies
```

### 5.5 Figure Scale

- Figure height should be roughly **8%–15%** of the image height;
- Present the family as a small-scale group;
- Figures must be recognizable but never become large portraits;
- Emphasize people acting within the farming world, not realistic facial detail.

### 5.6 Narrative Density

- Keep the visual weight of horizontal areas broadly comparable while varying density through foreground, middle-ground, and distance;
- Divide people, animals, tools, and landmarks into two or three mutually reinforcing groups;
- Do not pile every figure, animal, and tool into one zone or leave another zone purposelessly empty;
- Give each image one primary family action, with other actions supporting it;
- Keep the image readable instead of packing in every possible seasonal symbol.

## 6. Pure Illustration Output Rules

### 6.1 No Embedded Text

All 24 final images are pure illustrations. Do not add solar-term names, translations, pinyin, dates, sequence numbers, subtitles, signs, logos, signatures, or watermarks. Solar-term names belong in the website interface outside the image.

### 6.2 Composition Implications

- Do not reserve a fixed area for a title or web copy;
- Do not artificially suppress detail on one side;
- Distribute people, landmarks, animals, tools, and nature evenly across the width;
- Leave natural breathing room between elements, but do not create large purposeless blank areas.

## 7. Fixed Style Description

### 7.1 Fixed Style Paragraph

```text
This image belongs to a Chinese Twenty-Four Solar Terms illustration series created for an Agricola-themed project, and Klemens Franz, the original illustrator of Agricola, is explicitly acknowledged as a visual reference for the project. The artwork must be an original Chinese rural reinterpretation and must not reproduce any existing Agricola card, character, building, scene, or specific composition.

The entire series centers on the same Chinese farming family of five: a father, a mother, an elder daughter, a son, and a younger daughter. They are recurring characters throughout the full yearly cycle rather than random villagers in each image. Keep their age hierarchy, family roles, clothing style, and overall appearance consistent across the whole series so that viewers can clearly recognize the same family living through a full year of rural farming life. The fixed age hierarchy is: the elder daughter is the oldest child, the son is the middle child, and the younger daughter is the youngest.

Use a warm and rustic farming-board-game storytelling language: fine and naturally irregular pen linework, transparent watercolor washes, slightly uneven handmade coloring, ivory cold-press watercolor paper texture, small but clearly readable human figures, soft natural lighting, and muted natural-pigment colors. The work should acknowledge the classic pastoral board-game visual reference established by Klemens Franz for Agricola, while building an independent and original visual system grounded in traditional Chinese rural culture.
```

## 8. Spring Solar Terms

### 8.1 Start of Spring

**Main Palette**

- Tender shoot green;
- Thawing earth brown;
- Mist blue;
- Ivory.

**Weather and Environment**

- Winter gives way to spring;
- The ground has just begun to thaw;
- Fresh air;
- The first green willow shoots;
- A few early plum blossoms.

**Farming Activities**

- Organizing farm tools;
- Inspecting the wooden plow;
- Turning the surface soil;
- Selecting and sorting seeds;
- Preparing for spring plowing.

**Family Appearance and Roles**

- Father: inspects the wooden plow and draft animal;
- Mother: sorts seeds;
- Elder daughter: helps her mother with a bamboo basket;
- Son: follows his father and observes the plow or buffalo;
- Younger daughter: watches plum blossoms, poultry, or seed baskets beside the courtyard.

**Distinctive Chinese Elements**

- Plum blossoms;
- Willow shoots;
- Bamboo seed basket;
- Wooden plow;
- Water buffalo or ox.

**Composition**

- Place the father, son, and draft animal inspecting the plow at lower left, and the mother and daughters sorting seeds slightly right of center;
- Let thawing ground span the foreground, with houses, waterwheel, bridge, and willow forming balanced middle-ground anchors;
- Fill the upper area naturally with distant hills, tea slopes, and pale blue sky rather than deliberate blank space.

- Start of Spring: thawing and soil preparation;
- Rain Water: water collecting in channels;
- Awakening of Insects: plowing;
- Spring Equinox: sowing;
- Grain Rain: preparing seedling beds;
- Start of Summer: transplanting rice;
- Grain Buds: grain filling;

### 12.3 The Same Waterwheel Through the Year

- Spring: turns slowly in fine rain;
- Summer: runs frequently for irrigation;
- Autumn: water level falls and harvested crops collect nearby;

## 16. Core Project Summary

> **This series is not a simple replacement of Agricola’s European farm with Chinese buildings. It uses Agricola-like pastoral board-game storytelling to follow the same family of five through a complete year in the same Chinese farming village.**

The final work must combine:

- A recognizable recurring family of five;
- Credible solar-term and farming changes;
- Stable village landmarks;
- A consistent pen-and-watercolor board-game illustration language;
- Horizontally balanced, rhythmically spaced pure-illustration compositions;
- No embedded text, logos, signatures, or watermarks;
- A complete narrative loop from Start of Spring through Major Cold and back to Start of Spring.
